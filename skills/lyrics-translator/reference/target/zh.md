# Chinese Target Lyrics

## Output Rules

- Use concise, natural Chinese; omit pronouns and function words when they add no meaning.
- Convey tense, aspect, modality, and register only when semantically needed.
- Translate idioms, cultural terms, and sound words for meaning and musical effect. Avoid untranslated Japanese or Korean romanization.
- Preserve parallel hooks with comparable character counts where possible.

## Native Voice Checklist

Apply during review and cadence polish:

- Match the song's spoken register. For conversational youth lyrics, avoid bookish connectives and formal four-character expressions (`无济于事` / `并非` / `若要`); prefer spoken phrasing (`无论怎么努力都做不到`).
- Run a connotation check before locking any emotionally loaded word (`浮躁` = negative restlessness vs. `雀跃` / `心里痒痒的` = sweet giddiness — pick the color the source carries).
- Keep translationese out: no `的`-chains, no Europeanized passives (`被...`), no added logical connectives (`因此` / `所以`) absent from the source, and **no dangling line-ending `的`** when a modifier clause splits across timestamps — frame the first line as a self-contained scene/state clause and resume the noun on the following line.
- No invented parallel halves (`尝不出酸甜 摸不清好坏`) and no invented head nouns (`迷路的小孩`).

## Rhythm and Length

- **Canonical Chinese Length Cap**: Prefer **4–16 Chinese characters** per line; **18 characters is the hard ceiling**.
- Match beats and breath groups rather than translating every source syllable.
- Do not pad short lines with decorative modifiers. At the same time, **avoid weight collapse on multi-syllable hooks**: when a recurring title word or foreign loanword spans 5+ syllables/morae (e.g., `メタモルフォーゼ`), prefer an etymology-grounded 4-character expression (`破茧蜕变`) over a clipped 2-character gloss (`蜕变`) as long as the full line stays within the 18-character ceiling.
